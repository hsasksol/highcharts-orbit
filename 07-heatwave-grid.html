The hottest hour of the year

Texas has more people, more data centres and more air conditioning every summer, and one grid to carry all of it. This page follows a single question through live data: what does heat actually do to that grid, and what kept the lights on when it peaked? Every series is fetched from open APIs when you open the page. Use the Orbit toolbar on each chart, and the page bar at the top, to check each claim for yourself.

Background: ERCOT runs the grid for about 90 percent of Texas, on its own island with few connections to its neighbours. On 22 July 2026 it reported an all-time demand record of 91,308 MW at 5 pm local time, one day after breaking the previous record. The EIA hourly series used here shows 91,075 MWh for that hour. This page covers 18 June 2026 to today, hour by hour, and recalculates every number below from the data each time it loads.
