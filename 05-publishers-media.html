Use case 05 · Publishers / media

Why did this story attract a huge audience but so few subscribers?

A digital publisher monitors the performance of its articles. The ferry collision investigation received about three times the publisher's normal traffic but generated far fewer subscriptions than expected. Use the Orbit toolbar to understand where the traffic came from and how those readers behaved.

How to use Orbit

Step 1 of 6

Insights

The Ferry story stands out: about 3x the normal traffic but well below average subscription conversion.

How: Open the AI menu in the chart toolbar and choose Insights; Orbit writes up what changed and when.

Step 2 of 6

Compare

Its readership is far larger, but reading time and completion are much lower than other high-traffic stories.

How: Click a value in any chart to filter the whole page to it, or open Compare in the top bar to put two segments side by side.

Step 3 of 6

Filter and Focus

Most of the extra traffic came from social media: 612k of 1,044k pageviews.

How: Open Transform in the chart toolbar, then Filter and Focus, and narrow to the series you care about.

Step 4 of 6

Correlations

Reading time and completion predict subscriptions; raw pageviews do not. The bubble chart shows why.

How: Open Analyze in the chart toolbar, then Relationships, and run Correlations across the series.

Step 5 of 6

Contribution

Newsletter converts at 1.9% and Direct at 0.84%, while Social converts at 0.05%; the small audiences are the valuable ones.

How: Open the Contribution tool from the chart toolbar to rank how much each part makes up of the whole.

Step 6 of 6

Annotate

Mark the viral spike on the timeline and capture the finding for the rest of the team.

How: Open Annotate in the chart toolbar, then Draw, and mark the point directly on the chart.
